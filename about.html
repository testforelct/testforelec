About ELECTRANS

ELECTRANS is a research project funded by the French National Research Agency (ANR) and devoted to the 2027 French elections.

The project brings together researchers and institutions across the French electoral research community, including:

  • CEVIPOF – Sciences Po
  • Sciences Po Bordeaux
  • Sciences Po Grenoble
  • Université de Lille
  • Toulouse School of Economics
  • Progedo
  • CDSP – Sciences Po/CNRS

ELECTRANS will conduct several major surveys surrounding the 2027 French elections. Our goal is both to advance research on electoral behavior and political attitudes and to create a shared research infrastructure that can be used by the broader scientific community.

ELECTRANS is a collaborative infrastructure intended to strengthen cumulative research on elections in France.

Principal Investigators

KA

Kevin Arceneaux

Professor (FNSP) and Director of CEVIPOF, Sciences Po

Kevin Arceneaux studies how people form political beliefs and attitudes and how they make political choices. His research focuses on how social and psychological factors shape the media people consume, the opinions they defend, and the political behaviors they adopt. His interests include electoral behavior, psychological biases in political choice, and the effects of media and election campaigns.

DB

Damien Bol

Professor of Political Science, CEVIPOF, Sciences Po

Damien Bol works on comparative political behavior, examining how democratic institutions, and elections in particular, shape citizens’ attitudes and behavior, mainly through survey and experimental methods. He leads the French Electoral Studies Network (REEF), oversees the Political Trust Barometer, and coordinates the ELECTRANS project.

CC

Charlotte Cavaillé

Professor of Political Science, Toulouse School of Economics

Charlotte Cavaillé studies public attitudes toward redistributive social policies in a context of rising inequality, fiscal stress, and immigration. Her book Fair Enough? Support for Redistribution in the Age of Inequality (Cambridge University Press, 2023) received two best book awards from APSA sections.

FG

Florent Gougou

Associate Professor of Political Science, Sciences Po Grenoble – PACTE

Florent Gougou specializes in electoral studies and comparative politics, including the links between elections and ecological issues. In 2026–2027 he is on a CNRS research delegation at Progedo, studying major electoral realignments in Western Europe and how democracy functions.

Official page →
TH

Tristan Haute

Associate Professor of Political Science, Université de Lille – CERAPS

Tristan Haute works in electoral sociology, focusing on the social contexts of voting and abstention, workplace elections, and union engagement. He co-edited Extinction de vote ? (PUF, 2022) with Vincent Tiberj.

Official page →
NS

Nicolas Sauger

Professor of Political Science, Sciences Po – CDSP

Nicolas Sauger is a specialist in quantitative methods who has taken part in major data collection projects, including the European Social Survey, the Comparative Study of Electoral Systems, the French Election Study, and ELIPSS. His research examines how the structure of electoral competition has changed in France and Europe.

Official page →
VT

Vincent Tiberj

Professor, Sciences Po Bordeaux – Centre Émile Durkheim

Vincent Tiberj specializes in electoral and political behavior in France, Europe, and the United States, and in political psychology. His work covers how “ordinary” citizens reason about politics, social and ethnic inequalities in political life, and prejudice and value systems.
